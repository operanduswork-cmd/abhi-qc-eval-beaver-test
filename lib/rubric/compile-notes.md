# Rubric compilation notes

Every judgement made turning 60 KB of human-written rubric into two executable packs. The packs
are generated by `scripts/assemble-packs.mjs`; this file is why they look the way they do.

All `criteria` strings are **verbatim** from the source tables. The model reads the rubric's own
words, never a paraphrase.

---

## 1. Bucket vs range

Coaching is entirely discrete buckets — *"Each dimension's score must be exactly one of the
bucket values listed in its table. No interpolation."* All twelve compile directly.

Kick-off is a **hybrid**: seven dimensions are buckets, five are bands giving a span
(D1, D3, D5, D10, D12 — 35 of its 100 points). Those five are the reproducibility problem:
*"Strong = 6–8"* gives a model three legal answers and no tiebreak, so the same transcript
drifts across runs and the coach's band can flip.

## 2. How the five range dimensions were collapsed (decision D-03a)

For each band, descending:

**(a)** If the rubric's own text names specific values *with a rule for choosing between them*,
take every value it names. Example, D1 Strong: *"Score 8 when the gap is minor and clearly
outweighed by solid prep; 6 when prep is real but uneven."* → take 8 and 6.

**(b)** Otherwise take that band's **floor**, per Scoring Principle #4: *"score in the lower tier
of the band the call belongs to."*

**(c)** Except the **top** band, which always contributes its **ceiling** as well as its floor.

Rule (c) is the amendment, and it exists because rules (a)+(b) alone produced
**D5 = [9,8,6,3,1,0]**. D5's Elite band is 9–10 and names no tiebreak, so (b) took 9 — making
10/10 unreachable on D5 and capping the entire kick-off instrument at 99/100. Principle #4
governs *uncertainty when evidence is missing*; it was never meant to make a flawless call
unattainable. A dimension's stated maximum must be reachable or the rubric's own "out of 10" is
a fiction.

| | enum | derivation |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | `[10, 9, 8, 6, 4, 1, 0]` | Elite names 10 and 9 · Strong names 8 and 6 · Mid floor 4 · Weak floor 1 · Fail 0 |
| D3 | `[5, 4.5, 2.5, 1, 0]` | Elite 5 and 4.5 · then floors 2.5, 1, 0 |
| D5 | `[10, 9, 8, 6, 3, 1, 0]` | Elite ceiling 10 **(rule c)** + floor 9 · Strong names 8 and 6 · then floors |
| D10 | `[5, 4.5, 2.5, 1, 0]` | as D3 — the two have byte-identical bands in the source |
| D12 | `[5, 4.5, 3.5, 2, 1, 0]` | Elite 5 and 4.5 · then floors 3.5, 2, 1, 0 |

**On the value 7.** It appears in the end-of-document Calibration Anchors (*"Strong (7–8)"*,
*"Strong (6–7)"*) but never in D1's own tiebreak text, which names only 8 and 6. Anchors describe
where a call belongs, not how to choose within a band, so 7 is excluded. Recorded because it is
the most arguable exclusion in the set.

Every dropped value is one the rubric gives no rule for choosing. Enforcement is a numeric
JSON-Schema `enum` plus `additionalProperties:false` — probe M3 fired 20 adversarial attempts
demanding an excluded value and got **zero**.

## 3. Requirements — how "the dimension says so" was made structural

The brief states three times that a dimension must **say** when a behaviour is not in the
transcript. A dimension returning 3/10 with a prose paragraph has not complied — it has to name
the missing behaviour.

That is only possible if each required behaviour has its own slot, so each dimension's **top
bucket criteria are split at compile time into atomic requirements**, one per sentence. Coaching
D1 Elite becomes five:

```
D1.r1  Coach asks about body, wins, AND struggles.
D1.r2  Listens before responding — no interruption.
D1.r3  Reflects what they hear back to the client ("What I'm hearing is…").
D1.r4  States clear call intention tied to client's actual state.
D1.r5  Coach reads what kind of call is needed today and adjusts the approach accordingly.
```

The scorer reports evidence **per requirement** and code derives the state from whether the array
is empty. Absence becomes a property of the data rather than a hope about the prose. Splitting
happens at compile time, not per run, so the requirement list is byte-identical every run.

100 requirements total — 48 coaching, 52 kick-off.

## 4. The four evidence states (decision D-04)

| state | report wording | when |
|---|---|---|
| `present` | evidence, scored | verified quote found |
| `not_evidenced` | **"Not evidenced in the transcript"** | looked, found nothing |
| `contradicted` | `indeterminate`, quote BOTH sides, name the branch scored | evidence both ways |
| `unscoreable` | **"Cannot analyse — the transcript does not cover this"** | the format cannot carry the signal |

`not_evidenced` and `unscoreable` are **not** interchangeable, and conflating them costs points in
both directions. Saying *"cannot analyse"* about a coach who simply did not book the next call
hands them a free pass on a `0/5 non-recoverable` — precisely the failure `coaching-01` exists to
catch. Saying *"did not happen"* about D12's pacing penalises the coach for our instrument's
blindness.

Wording is deliberate: *"not evidenced in the transcript"*, never *"did not happen"*. The
transcript is all we ever see; the coach may have done it off-recording. It still scores as
absent — the report just does not overclaim.

## 5. What is genuinely unscoreable — one thing

**Only `coaching D12`'s SOP minute allocations.** Verified: zero timestamps across all four
fixtures. Pacing is scored from a documented words-and-turns proxy and the report says so.

Earlier drafts also listed interruption, live movement and listening pauses. **All three were
wrong**, caught by the adversarial audit and confirmed by grepping the corpus:

- **42 non-verbal markers exist**: `[laughs]`×20, `[inaudible]`×8, `[pause]`×3, `[exertion]`×3,
  `[breathing]`×2, `[stepping]`-family×4, `[shuffling]`-family×2.
- **Turns are not strictly alternating** — eight same-speaker continuations across three of the
  four fixtures.
- **Interruption is explicitly acknowledged**: `coaching-02` L243 *"sorry go on, you were
  saying—"* / L244 *"No no, go on, **I interrupted you**—"*, plus em-dash cut-offs in every file.

So coaching D1's *"Listens before responding — no interruption"* is **scorable**, and D4's
detection criterion 1 has **direct** evidence rather than only narration. `parse.ts` exposes
`movementLines` and `cutOffLines` for exactly this.

Do not grow the unscoreable set without grepping the corpus first.

## 6. D4 — the four-vs-five detection discrepancy

The prose lists **five** exclusions (adding *"no in-call demonstration"*); the numbered
*"Detection criteria — ALL four must be absent to disable"* list has **four**. The four numbered
criteria are treated as normative and the discrepancy is surfaced in `arithmeticNotes`.

Checked against the fixtures: `coaching-01` carries eleven movement markers at L48–L86, so D4 is
**active** there. `coaching-02`'s only four markers are all `[inaudible]` audio dropouts, so D4 is
**disabled** — the eval target survives.

## 7. Cache grouping

Probe M2b established that a schema's **enum** invalidates the prompt cache but its **name** does
not. So all dimensions share one schema name and the calls are ordered by enum group.

- **Coaching: 4 distinct enums** across 12 dimensions — `[5,3,0]` covers D7,D8,D9,D11,D12 ·
  `[10,7,3,0]` covers D1,D2,D5 · `[15,10,5,0]` covers D3,D4,D6 · `[5,0]` is D10 alone.
  Four cache misses, not twelve.
- **Kick-off: 7.**

Collapsing to a single universal schema would halve the cost but requires the enum to be the
union of all values, which puts `7` back in range. M3 proved the hard enum is what stops
interpolation, so the cost stands.

## 8. Defaults — missing is not zero

`D8 → 5/5` when no struggle arose · `D5 → 7/10` when no adjustments were needed · `D2 → N/A`,
never 0. On evidence failure the score **clamps** to `defaultWhenAbsent` rather than flooring to
0; flooring is harsher than the source document and punishes a smooth call.
